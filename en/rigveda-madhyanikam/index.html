Ṛgveda · Āśvalāyana sūtra · Smārta

Follow the English ritual instructions and recite the Sanskrit mantras in Roman transliteration.

This follows the selected family procedure on this site. Counts, posture, nyāsa and some readings vary: follow your guru’s instruction.

How to pronounce the Sanskrit

The mantra uses IAST, a precise Roman transliteration of Sanskrit. Read the marked letters as Sanskrit sounds. Recite the Sanskrit block.

  • a / ā, i / ī, u / ū: short / long vowels. Hold ā, ī and ū about twice as long. Keep e and o pure and long; ai and au are diphthongs.
  • ṛ: a syllabic r, not the English word “ree.” ṝ is its long counterpart. ḷ in the Rigvedic mṛḷaya is a retroflex lateral consonant.
  • c: like ch in “church”; j: like j in “judge.” ś: a palatal sh; ṣ: sh with the tongue curled back; s: a dental s.
  • ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh, ṇ: curl the tongue back. For t, th, d, dh and n, place it near the upper teeth.
  • kh, gh, ch, jh, th, dh, ph, bh: one consonant released with a breath. Sanskrit th is not English “thin,” and ph is not f. Keep both sounds in clusters such as kṣ and jñ.
  • ṅ: ng as in “sing”; ñ: a palatal nasal. ṃ: anusvāra, a nasal sound whose realization depends on the next sound and the recitation tradition. ḥ: visarga, a breath after a vowel; its linked pronunciation depends on what follows.
  • ' marks an elided a, not a pause. Spaces help reading; do not insert a pause at every space. For example, bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ is linked as bhūr bhuvas suvaḥ. Double consonants are held longer. The bars | and || mark textual divisions.

Vedic svara: pitch accents and special Vedic nasal realizations are not encoded here. Use the oral recitation taught by your guru for pitch, breath, and connected chanting. IAST alone cannot supply those features.

9. Arghya — offering water

Stand facing east. Hold water in both cupped palms. Recite the complete mantra and offer the water 2 times, reciting once for each offering. Release the water into a clean place or the receiving vessel.

Sanskrit · IAST

oṃ bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ |
tat savitur vareṇyaṃ bhargo devasya dhīmahi |
dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt ||

The Sāvitrī verse addresses Savitṛ, the divine impeller; Ṛgveda 3.62.10. The offering count follows this guide’s selected practice.

31. Salutations to the Sandhyā deities

Turn clockwise through east, south, west, north, joining the palms in each direction. Then face east.

32. Abhivādana — stating lineage and saluting

Face east. Supply the pravara sages, their count, gotra and personal name exactly as taught in your family. Do not recite the bracketed English prompts. Then bow in the manner taught.

Sanskrit · IAST

abhivādaye [your pravara sages] [your pravara count] pravarānvitaḥ |
[your gotra] gotraḥ āśvalāyana sūtraḥ ṛkśākhādhyāyī |
[your name] śarmā nāmāham asmi bhoḥ ||

The personal fields are not guessed. Use your own instructed lineage formula.

Stand facing east, palms joined.

Repeat abhivādana and bow. Then repeat ācamana and aṅgavandana, from Acyuta through Dāmodara.

38. Samarpaṇa — offering all actions

Sit facing east. Hold a little water, recite, and release it at “brahmārpaṇam astu.”

Sanskrit · IAST

kāyena vācā manasendriyair vā buddhyātmanā vā prakṛteḥ svabhāvāt |
karomi yad yat sakalaṃ parasmai nārāyaṇāyeti samarpayāmi ||
oṃ tat sat brahmārpaṇam astu |

Then repeat ācamana and aṅgavandana, from Acyuta through Dāmodara.

39. Japasthāna prokṣaṇa — completing the practice

Sprinkle a little water on the place where you sat for japa. Touch it with the ring finger, recite these prayers and place a water mark on the forehead.

Sanskrit · IAST

adyā no deva savitaḥ prajāvat sāvīḥ saubhagam | parā duḥṣvapnyaṃ suva ||
viśvāni deva savitar duritāni parā suva | yad bhadraṃ tan na ā suva ||

This completes Rigveda Mādhyāhnika Sandhyāvandanam.
