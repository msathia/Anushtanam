Rigveda Mādhyāhnika Sandhyāvandanam

Ṛgveda · Āśvalāyana sūtra · Smārta

English instructions, Sanskrit in Roman transliteration, and close English meanings for every recitation. Read the meaning first if helpful; recite the Sanskrit text.

This follows the selected family procedure on this site. Counts, posture, nyāsa and some readings vary: follow your guru’s instruction.

How to pronounce the Sanskrit

The mantra uses IAST, a precise Roman transliteration of Sanskrit. Read the marked letters as Sanskrit sounds. The English meaning is for understanding; recite the Sanskrit block.

  • a / ā, i / ī, u / ū: short / long vowels. Hold ā, ī and ū about twice as long. Keep e and o pure and long; ai and au are diphthongs.
  • ṛ: a syllabic r, not the English word “ree.” ṝ is its long counterpart. ḷ in the Rigvedic mṛḷaya is a retroflex lateral consonant.
  • c: like ch in “church”; j: like j in “judge.” ś: a palatal sh; ṣ: sh with the tongue curled back; s: a dental s.
  • ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh, ṇ: curl the tongue back. For t, th, d, dh and n, place it near the upper teeth.
  • kh, gh, ch, jh, th, dh, ph, bh: one consonant released with a breath. Sanskrit th is not English “thin,” and ph is not f. Keep both sounds in clusters such as kṣ and jñ.
  • ṅ: ng as in “sing”; ñ: a palatal nasal. ṃ: anusvāra, a nasal sound whose realization depends on the next sound and the recitation tradition. ḥ: visarga, a breath after a vowel; its linked pronunciation depends on what follows.
  • ' marks an elided a, not a pause. Spaces help reading; do not insert a pause at every space. For example, bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ is linked as bhūr bhuvas suvaḥ. Double consonants are held longer. The bars | and || mark textual divisions.

Vedic svara: pitch accents and special Vedic nasal realizations are not encoded here. Use the oral recitation taught by your guru for pitch, breath, and connected chanting. IAST alone cannot supply those features.

1. Ācamana — sipping water

Sit facing east. Place a small quantity of clean water in the right palm and sip once for each of these three names.

Sanskrit · IAST

acyutāya namaḥ | anantāya namaḥ | govindāya namaḥ |

Close English meaning

Salutation to Acyuta, the unfailing one; to Ananta, the endless one; to Govinda, protector of cows.

Wipe the lips and clean the hand as taught. Begin aṅgavandana by saying the first four names and touching the indicated places with the right hand.

Name to reciteFingerTouch
KeśavaThumbRight cheek
NārāyaṇaThumbLeft cheek
MādhavaRing fingerRight eye
GovindaRing fingerLeft eye

Name meanings: Keśava: the beautiful-haired one; Nārāyaṇa: refuge of beings; Mādhava: consort of Mā (Lakṣmī); Govinda: protector of cows.

2. Aṅgavandana — touching the limbs

Continue with the remaining eight names, using the right hand.

Name to reciteFingerTouch

VāmanaLittle fingerLeft ear
ŚrīdharaMiddle fingerRight shoulder
HṛṣīkeśaMiddle fingerLeft shoulder
PadmanābhaFour fingersChest
DāmodaraAll five fingersHead

Name meanings: Viṣṇu: the pervading one; Madhusūdana: slayer of Madhu; Trivikrama: the one of three strides; Vāmana: the dwarf; Śrīdhara: bearer of Śrī; Hṛṣīkeśa: lord of the senses; Padmanābha: lotus-navelled one; Dāmodara: the one with a cord around his belly. These are traditional meanings of Viṣṇu’s names; several names have more than one interpretation.

3. Gaṇapati meditation

Sanskrit · IAST

śuklāmbaradharaṃ viṣṇuṃ śaśivarṇaṃ caturbhujam |
prasannavadanaṃ dhyāyet sarvavighnopaśāntaye ||

Close English meaning

One should meditate on the all-pervading one, clothed in white, moon-coloured, four-armed and serene-faced, for the calming of every obstacle.

Gently tap both sides of the forehead five times with the hands, following the method taught.

A meditation verse used here for Gaṇapati; viṣṇum in this verse means “all-pervading.”

4. Prāṇāyāma — regulated breath

Use the inhalation, retention and exhalation method taught by your guru.

Sanskrit · IAST

oṃ bhūḥ oṃ bhuvaḥ oṃ svaḥ oṃ mahaḥ oṃ janaḥ oṃ tapaḥ oṃ satyam |
oṃ tat savitur vareṇyaṃ bhargo devasya dhīmahi |
dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt ||
oṃ āpo jyotī raso ’mṛtaṃ brahma bhūr bhuvaḥ svar om ||

Close English meaning

Oṃ: Bhūḥ, Bhuvaḥ, Svaḥ (heaven), Mahaḥ, Janaḥ, Tapaḥ, Satyam—the seven sacred utterances, naming the worlds. We meditate on that worthy-of-choice radiance of the god Savitṛ. May he impel our thoughts. Oṃ. Waters, light, essence, immortality—all are brahman. Earth, the intermediate realm, heaven: Oṃ.

Touch the right ear at the end.

5. Saṅkalpa — intention for this worship

Sit facing east. Rest the right hand over the left palm on the right thigh and state the intention.

Sanskrit · IAST

mamopātta samasta duritakṣayadvārā śrī parameśvara prītyartham |
mādhyāhnika sandhyām upāsiṣye |

Close English meaning

For the removal of all wrongs incurred by me, and for the pleasure of the supreme Lord, I shall perform the noon Sandhyā worship.

Then touch water with the ring finger and place a water mark on the forehead while reciting:

Sanskrit · IAST

oṃ śrī keśavāya namaḥ |

Close English meaning

Oṃ. Salutation to the blessed Keśava.

6. Mārjana — sprinkling water

For nyāsa, touch the head when naming the seer (ṛṣi), the tip of the nose for the metre (chandas), and the chest for the deity (devatā), as taught.

Sanskrit · IAST

āpohiṣṭheti tṛcasya sindhudvīpa ṛṣiḥ | gāyatrī chandaḥ | āpo devatā | apāṃ prokṣaṇe viniyogaḥ |

Close English meaning

For the three verses beginning Āpo hi ṣṭhā: Sindhudvīpa is the seer, Gāyatrī the metre, and the Waters the deity. They are used for sprinkling water.

Āpo hi ṣṭhā: a prayer to the Waters, also found in Ṛgveda 10.9.1–3.

Sprinkle water on the head with the ring finger for the first seven phrases; at “yasya kṣayāya jinvatha” sprinkle the feet, and at “āpo janayathā ca naḥ” sprinkle the head again.

Sanskrit · IAST

āpo hi ṣṭhā mayobhuvaḥ | tā na ūrje dadhātana | mahe raṇāya cakṣase ||
yo vaḥ śivatamo rasaḥ | tasya bhājayateha naḥ | uśatīr iva mātaraḥ ||
tasmā araṃ gamāma vaḥ | yasya kṣayāya jinvatha | āpo janayathā ca naḥ ||

Close English meaning

Waters, you indeed bring well-being. Give us strength, for great delight and vision. Let us share here in your most auspicious essence, like loving mothers. May we readily approach that dwelling of yours to which you quicken us; waters, bring us into renewed life.

Sanskrit · IAST

oṃ bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ |

Close English meaning

Oṃ. Earth, the intermediate realm, heaven: the three sacred utterances.

Circle the head clockwise with water, as taught.

7. Mantra prāśana — purification by sipping

For nyāsa, touch the head when naming the seer (ṛṣi), the tip of the nose for the metre (chandas), and the chest for the deity (devatā), as taught.

Sanskrit · IAST

āpaḥ punantv ity anuvākasya viśvedevā ṛṣayaḥ | anuṣṭup chandaḥ | āpo devatā | apāṃ prāśane viniyogaḥ |

Close English meaning

For the Āpaḥ punantu passage: the All-gods are the seers, Anuṣṭubh the metre, and the Waters the deity. It is used for sipping water.

Hold a little water in the right palm. Recite the entire mantra, then sip the water.

Sanskrit · IAST

āpaḥ punantu pṛthivīṃ pṛthivī pūtā punātu mām |
punantu brahmaṇaspatiḥ brahma pūtā punātu mām |
yad ucchiṣṭam abhojyaṃ yad vā duścaritaṃ mama |
sarvaṃ punantu mām āpo 'satāṃ ca pratigrahaṃ svāhā ||

Close English meaning

May the waters purify the earth; may the purified earth purify me. May the Lord of sacred utterance purify; may the earth, purified by brahman, purify me. Whatever leftover or forbidden food I have eaten, whatever misconduct is mine, and acceptance of gifts from the unworthy—may the waters cleanse me of it all. Svāhā. “Earth” in the second sentence is supplied from the preceding line; brahman here can be understood as sacred knowledge or utterance.

The period-specific text is transmitted in the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka / Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad tradition. Manyu denotes anger or wrath, also personified as a divine power.

Then repeat ācamana and aṅgavandana, from Acyuta through Dāmodara.

9. Arghya — offering water

Stand facing east. Hold water in both cupped palms. Recite the complete mantra and offer the water 2 times, reciting once for each offering. Release the water into a clean place or the receiving vessel.

Sanskrit · IAST

oṃ bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ |
tat savitur vareṇyaṃ bhargo devasya dhīmahi |
dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt ||

Close English meaning

Oṃ. Earth, the intermediate realm, heaven: the three sacred utterances. We meditate on that worthy-of-choice radiance of the god Savitṛ. May he impel our thoughts.

The Sāvitrī verse addresses Savitṛ, the divine impeller; Ṛgveda 3.62.10. The offering count follows this guide’s selected practice.

Close English meaning

13. Ātmānusandhāna — contemplation of the Self

Sit facing east. Contemplate the sun as brahman and yourself as brahman.

Sanskrit · IAST

asāv ādityo brahma brahmaivāham asmi |

Close English meaning

That sun is brahman. I myself am brahman.

Then repeat ācamana and aṅgavandana, from Acyuta through Dāmodara.

Close English meaning

“Tarpayāmi” means “I satisfy with an offering of water.” The first nine offerings are to Āditya (Sun), Soma (Moon), Aṅgāraka (Mars), Budha (Mercury), Bṛhaspati (Jupiter), Śukra (Venus), Śanaiścara (Saturn), Rāhu and Ketu (the lunar nodes). The remaining twelve are to the forms of Viṣṇu named in aṅgavandana above, in the same order.

Then repeat ācamana and aṅgavandana, from Acyuta through Dāmodara.

15. Prayer

Pray facing east.

Sanskrit · IAST

namo brahmaṇyadevāya gobrāhmaṇahitāya ca |
jagaddhitāya kṛṣṇāya govindāya namo namaḥ ||
ā brahmalokād āśeṣād ālokālokaparvatāt |
ye vasanti dvijā devās tebhyo nityaṃ namo namaḥ ||

Close English meaning

Salutation to the god devoted to sacred learning, benefactor of cows and Brahmins; repeated salutation to Kṛṣṇa, Govinda, benefactor of the world. To the twice-born and divine beings dwelling throughout the realms, as far as Brahmā’s world and the Lokāloka mountain, my constant salutations.

22. Gāyatrī āvāhana — invocation

gāyatrīṃ varadābhayāṅkuśakaśāḥ śubhraṃ kapālaṃ gadāṃ |
śaṅkhaṃ cakram athāravindayugalaṃ hastair vahantīṃ bhaje ||
yo devaḥ savitāsmākaṃ dhiyo dharmādigocarāḥ |
prerayet tasya yad bhargas tad vareṇyam upāsmahe ||

Close English meaning

I worship Gāyatrī, whose three-eyed faces have the hues of pearl, coral, gold, blue and white; whose jewelled crown bears the crescent moon; whose syllables embody the meaning of the principles of reality. Her hands show the gestures of granting boons and freedom from fear, and carry a goad, whip, white skull-bowl, mace, conch, discus and a pair of lotuses. We worship that choice radiance of the god Savitṛ who may impel our thoughts towards dharma and the other aims of life.

These are meditation verses, distinct from the Vedic Sāvitrī mantra that follows.

25. Gāyatrī mantra japa

Sit facing east in the posture taught by your guru. This guide uses 32 repetitions for noon; follow the count given in your instruction.

Sanskrit · IAST

oṃ bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ |
tat savitur vareṇyaṃ bhargo devasya dhīmahi |
dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt ||

Close English meaning

Oṃ. Earth, the intermediate realm, heaven: the three sacred utterances. We meditate on that worthy-of-choice radiance of the god Savitṛ. May he impel our thoughts.

Seer: Viśvāmitra. Metre: Nicṛd Gāyatrī. Deity: Savitṛ. The English meaning is not part of the recitation.

27. Prāṇāyāma — regulated breath

Use the inhalation, retention and exhalation method taught by your guru.

Sanskrit · IAST

oṃ bhūḥ oṃ bhuvaḥ oṃ svaḥ oṃ mahaḥ oṃ janaḥ oṃ tapaḥ oṃ satyam |
oṃ tat savitur vareṇyaṃ bhargo devasya dhīmahi |

Close English meaning

Oṃ: Bhūḥ, Bhuvaḥ, Svaḥ (heaven), Mahaḥ, Janaḥ, Tapaḥ, Satyam—the seven sacred utterances, naming the worlds. We meditate on that worthy-of-choice radiance of the god Savitṛ. May he impel our thoughts. Oṃ. Waters, light, essence, immortality—all are brahman. Earth, the intermediate realm, heaven: Oṃ.

28. Upasthāna intention and farewell to Gāyatrī

Sanskrit · IAST

mamopātta samasta duritakṣayadvārā śrī parameśvara prītyartham |
mādhyāhnika sandhyā upasthānaṃ kariṣye |

Close English meaning

For the removal of all wrongs incurred by me, and for the pleasure of the supreme Lord, I shall perform the standing attendance of the noon Sandhyā.

Sanskrit · IAST

uttama ity anuvākasya vāmadeva ṛṣiḥ | anuṣṭup chandaḥ | gāyatrī devatā | gāyatrī udvāsane viniyogaḥ |

Close English meaning

For the passage beginning Uttame: Vāmadeva is the seer, Anuṣṭubh the metre, and Gāyatrī the deity. It is used for bidding farewell to Gāyatrī.

Stand, bend to touch the earth with the ring finger, recite the farewell, then stand upright.

Sanskrit · IAST

uttame śikhare devi bhūmyāṃ parvatamūrdhani |
brāhmaṇebhyo hy anujñānaṃ gaccha devi yathāsukham ||

Close English meaning

Goddess, to the highest peak, to the mountain summit on earth, go at your pleasure, with leave from the Brahmins. This gives the sense of the ritual farewell reading; other editions have abhyanujñātā (“having been given leave”).

29. Sūrya upasthāna — standing prayer

Stand facing east, with palms joined, and recite the passages in order.

For nyāsa, touch the head when naming the seer (ṛṣi), the tip of the nose for the metre (chandas), and the chest for the deity (devatā), as taught.

Turn clockwise through east, south, west, north, joining the palms in each direction. Then face east.

RecitationDirection
sandhyāyai namaḥ |East
sāvitryai namaḥ |South
gāyatryai namaḥ |West
sarasvatyai namaḥ |North

Sanskrit · IAST

sarvābhyo devatābhyo namo namaḥ |
kāmo ’kārṣīn manyur akārṣīt namo namaḥ |

Close English meaning

Repeated salutations to all the deities. Desire did it; anger did it: repeated salutations. This acknowledges the impulses behind wrongdoing.

34. Yama vandana

Stand facing south.

Sanskrit · IAST

yamāya dharmarājāya mṛtyave cāntakāya ca |
vaivasvatāya kālāya sarvabhūtakṣayāya ca ||
audumbarāya dadhnāya nīlāya parameṣṭhine |
vṛkodarāya citrāya citraguptāya vai namaḥ ||
citraguptāya vai nama om nama iti |

Close English meaning

Salutation to Yama, king of dharma; to Death and the Ender; to the son of Vivasvat, Time, and the one who brings all beings to their end; to Audumbara, Dadhna, the dark one, the supremely stationed one, the wolf-bellied one, Citra and Citragupta. Salutation again to Citragupta. Oṃ, salutation. Audumbara and Dadhna are retained as traditional names.

35. Harihara vandana

Close English meaning

Repeated salutation to the one whose form is the universe: sacred order, truth, supreme brahman, the Person, dark and tawny, whose generative power rises upward, whose eyes are distinctive. Salutation to the universal form. Oṃ, salutation.

If this prayer belongs to your family’s practice, stand facing west and recite.

May he protect me from serpents. Oṃ, salutation.

37. Sūryanārāyaṇa vandana

Stand facing east, palms joined.

Sanskrit · IAST

namaḥ savitre jagadekacakṣuṣe |
jagatprasūtisthitināśahetave |
trayīmayāya triguṇātmadhāriṇe |
viriñcinārāyaṇaśaṅkarātmane ||
dhyeyaḥ sadā savitṛmaṇḍalamadhyavartī |
nārāyaṇaḥ sarasijāsanasanniviṣṭaḥ |
keyūravān makarakuṇḍalavān kirīṭī |
hārī hiraṇmayavapur dhṛtaśaṅkhacakraḥ ||
śaṅkhacakragadāpāṇe dvārakānilayācyuta |
govinda puṇḍarīkākṣa rakṣa māṃ śaraṇāgatam ||
ākāśāt patitaṃ toyaṃ yathā gacchati sāgaram |
sarvadevanamaskāraḥ keśavaṃ prati gacchati ||
keśavaṃ prati gacchatv om nama iti |

Close English meaning

Salutation to Savitṛ, the world’s single eye, the cause of its birth, continuance and dissolution; embodying the three Vedas and bearing the three qualities; having the nature of Viriñci (Brahmā), Nārāyaṇa and Śaṅkara. Always meditate on Nārāyaṇa in the centre of the solar disc, seated on a lotus, with armlets, makara-shaped earrings, crown and necklace, a golden body, and conch and discus in his hands. Unfailing one dwelling in Dvārakā, bearer of conch, discus and mace, Govinda, lotus-eyed one, protect me who has sought refuge. As water fallen from the sky reaches the ocean, salutation to every deity reaches Keśava. May it reach Keśava. Oṃ, salutation.

Repeat abhivādana and bow. Then repeat ācamana and aṅgavandana, from Acyuta through Dāmodara.

38. Samarpaṇa — offering all actions

Sit facing east. Hold a little water, recite, and release it at “brahmārpaṇam astu.”

Sanskrit · IAST

kāyena vācā manasendriyair vā buddhyātmanā vā prakṛteḥ svabhāvāt |
karomi yad yat sakalaṃ parasmai nārāyaṇāyeti samarpayāmi ||
oṃ tat sat brahmārpaṇam astu |

Close English meaning

Whatever I do through body, speech, mind or senses, through understanding or self, or by the disposition of nature, I offer it all to the supreme Nārāyaṇa. Oṃ, That, the Real; may this be an offering to brahman.

Then repeat ācamana and aṅgavandana, from Acyuta through Dāmodara.

39. Japasthāna prokṣaṇa — completing the practice

Sprinkle a little water on the place where you sat for japa. Touch it with the ring finger, recite these prayers and place a water mark on the forehead.

Sanskrit · IAST

adyā no deva savitaḥ prajāvat sāvīḥ saubhagam | parā duḥṣvapnyaṃ suva ||
viśvāni deva savitar duritāni parā suva | yad bhadraṃ tan na ā suva ||

Close English meaning

God Savitṛ, bring us good fortune today, with offspring; drive away bad dreams. God Savitṛ, drive away all evils; bring what is good to us.

This completes Rigveda Mādhyāhnika Sandhyāvandanam.
